# mock-drill.md — running the mock interview + question sets

## Question sets by type (pull what the role needs)

**The classics (almost every interview):**
- Tell me about yourself. / Walk me through your background.
- Why this role? Why this company? Why now? Why leaving?
- Greatest strength. Greatest weakness (a real one + what you're doing about it).
- Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years? (ties to `personal/career-roadmap.md`)
- What are your salary expectations? → **route to the `negotiation` skill; don't wing it.**
- Do you have questions for us? (never "no" — see below)

**Behavioral (STAR — map to the story bank):**
- Tell me about a time you led… / influenced without authority… / disagreed with your boss…
- …failed / made a mistake… / worked with ambiguity… / delivered under a hard deadline…
- …handled a conflict… / made a call with incomplete data… / drove a turnaround…
- "Your biggest professional accomplishment." / "A time you changed someone's mind."

**Role-specific / technical / domain:**
- Derive from the JD's must-haves. For senior/leadership: operating model, strategy,
  scope, org design, stakeholder management, metrics they'd own, how they'd approach the
  first 90 days. For hands-on: the actual craft questions the JD implies.

**Level calibration:**
- **Recruiter screen** → fit, motivation, comp range, logistics; keep answers tight.
- **Hiring manager** → the behavioral + role-specific core; this is where stories win.
- **Panel / cross-functional** → breadth, collaboration, how peers experience you.
- **Executive / final** → vision, judgment, "why you," culture; fewer STARs, more perspective.

## The feedback rubric (score each mock answer against these)
1. **STAR complete?** Situation and Task brief; **Action is theirs and specific**; Result lands.
2. **Specific + true?** Real detail and numbers (from the profile), not generalities or invented figures.
3. **Length?** ~60–120s. Rambling loses the room; a two-sentence answer looks thin. Flag both.
4. **The "so what"?** Did the business outcome / lesson land, or did it trail off?
5. **First person?** "I", not "we", for the action inside a team win.
6. **Filler / hedging?** "Um", "kind of", "I guess", over-qualifying. Note patterns, don't nitpick once.
7. **Answered the actual question?** Behavioral answers drift into unrelated stories — check the match.

Give feedback candidly but kindly: name the one or two highest-leverage fixes, have them
redo it, and confirm it improved. Escalate — add a follow-up probe ("what was your exact
role?", "what would you do differently?", "walk me through the numbers"), then pushback,
as they get stronger. The goal is a better answer next time, not a grade.

## Questions to ask THEM (prep 4–6, level-appropriate)
Sharp questions signal seriousness and screen the role. Examples to tailor:
- What does success look like in this role at 6 and 12 months?
- What's the biggest challenge facing the team right now?
- How is this team/function measured? What would I own?
- Why is the role open? / What happened to the last person in it?
- (Exec) How does leadership think about {the function's} maturity and investment?
Avoid questions answered on the careers page; avoid leading with comp/perks.

## Post-interview debrief (capture immediately after)
- What was actually asked (add new question types to the bank).
- What landed / what didn't (which stories worked, where you stumbled).
- Follow-up owed (thank-you within 24h, any materials promised).
- Read on fit and next step. Feed all of this into the next prep pack — prep compounds.
