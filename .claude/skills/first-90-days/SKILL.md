# First 90 Days (Landing Spoke)

> **Coach voice:** adopt the tone set by `userconfig.COACH_VOICE` (see `coach-voice.md`) — supportive / tough-love / zen / humorous / analytical. It changes **delivery only**; the honest substance, the real gaps, and the no-fabrication rule never change.

The search doesn't end at "yes" — a rocky ramp can undo the whole move. This turns a
new role into a strong start: a deliberate plan to learn, build trust, and land early
wins before the honeymoon ends. Doubles as prep for the "how would you approach your
first 90 days?" interview question.

> **Where files live (READ THIS):** every user-specific file lives in the gitignored `personal/` folder. It reads `personal/career-profile.md` and `personal/career-roadmap.md`; it writes `personal/applications/<company>/first-90-days.md`. Read from and write to `personal/`.

## The shape: learn → win → lead (30 / 60 / 90)
Calibrate depth to **level and mandate** — a turnaround differs from a scale role differs
from a 0→1 (read the JD and what they told you in interviews). Details in `references/plan.md`.

- **Days 1–30 — Learn & listen.** Understand the business, the metrics, the systems, the
  history, and *why things are the way they are* before changing anything. Meet everyone who
  matters; ask, don't assert. Resist the urge to "prove yourself" by breaking things early.
- **Days 31–60 — Diagnose & a first win.** Form a point of view backed by what you learned;
  ship one **visible, low-risk win** that builds trust and shows you're here to help, not
  reorganize on day 3. Align your read with your manager.
- **Days 61–90 — Set direction.** Turn the diagnosis into a plan/operating model the team and
  your manager buy into; start executing. By day 90 you should own a clear "here's where we're
  going and why."

## Across all three: four tracks
1. **Business & domain** — how it makes money, the metrics you'll own, the real problems.
2. **Relationships** — manager (expectations + how they like to work), peers, your team
   (1:1s early), and cross-functional stakeholders. Trust is the currency of the first 90.
3. **Early wins** — pick 1–2 that are visible, achievable in the window, and genuinely helpful
   (not vanity). Under-promise here.
4. **Operating rhythm** — how you'll run: cadences, how you'll communicate up, what "good"
   looks like, quick governance/setup so you're not firefighting at day 89.

## Use it two ways
- **Post-offer (the real thing):** build it in week one; review with your manager early — asking
  "here's my draft 90-day plan, what would you add/reprioritize?" is itself a trust-builder.
- **Interview asset:** a crisp, humble, learn-first 90-day answer signals maturity. `interview-prep`
  can pull from this for the "first 90 days" question.

## Guardrails
- **Learn before you change.** The fastest way to fail a senior role is reorganizing before you
  understand why it's the way it is. Diagnosis precedes prescription.
- **Manager alignment is the plan.** Their definition of success in this role beats a generic template.
- **Small, real wins.** Visible and helpful, not flashy and disruptive.
- **Calibrate to the role.** Level, mandate (turnaround/scale/build), and culture change everything — don't paste a template.
