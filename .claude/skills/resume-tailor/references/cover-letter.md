# Cover Letter

Short, specific, built entirely on genuine fit. A cover letter earns its place only if it says something the resume can't — usually *why this company/role specifically* and how the candidate's real pattern maps to what they need. If it could be sent to any company with the name swapped, it's worthless.

## Structure (roughly 4 short paragraphs, ~250-350 words)
1. **The hook — why this role, this company, now.** One or two sentences that show the candidate actually understands what this company is doing and why they're a fit for *this* thesis (build / scale / turnaround / governance — pull it from the target analysis). No "I am writing to express my interest in..."
2. **Proof point 1** — the single most relevant thing from the inventory, told as a compact story with a real result. Map it explicitly to a need in the JD.
3. **Proof point 2 (and maybe 3)** — a second dimension of fit (e.g., first para was technical/platform, this one is leadership/stakeholder). Keep it concrete and real.
4. **Forward-looking close** — what the candidate would focus on or bring in the first stretch, framed around the company's stated priorities. Confident, not presumptuous. Clear ask for a conversation.

## Rules
- **Every claim traces to the inventory.** Same honesty rule as the resume. No invented enthusiasm-driven specifics.
- **Mirror their language** the same way the resume does — their words for the role and the problems.
- **Cut the boilerplate openers and closers.** "I am writing to express," "I believe I would be a great fit," "Thank you for your time and consideration" — all deletable.
- **One voice.** It should sound like the same human who'll show up to the interview, not like a template.
- **Leadership tone:** at senior levels the letter reads as a peer talking to a hiring exec about their problem, not a supplicant. Match that register without arrogance.

## De-AI applies here too
Run the letter through `de-ai-checklist.md`. Cover letters are where AI tells cluster worst (tricolons, "passionate about," "thrilled at the opportunity," uniform sentence length). Break them.
