# Informational Interview (Relationship Spoke)

> **Coach voice:** adopt the tone set by `userconfig.COACH_VOICE` (see `coach-voice.md`) — supportive / tough-love / zen / humorous / analytical. It changes **delivery only**; the honest substance, the real gaps, and the no-fabrication rule never change.

The most underused, highest-yield channel — especially senior. You're not asking for a
job (that makes people defensive); you're asking to *learn*, which most people say yes to.
Done well, it turns strangers into advocates who think of you when a role opens. Where
`network` writes the message, this owns the *strategy and the conversation*.

> **Where files live (READ THIS):** every user-specific file lives in the gitignored `personal/` folder. It reads `personal/career-profile.md` and `personal/reconnect-list.md`; it logs to `personal/informational-interviews.md`. Read from and write to `personal/`.

## The golden rule
**Never ask for a job in an informational interview.** Ask to learn — about the company, the
role, the path, the industry, their story. The referral/opportunity, when it comes, comes
*because you didn't ask* and made a genuine connection. Asking for a job turns a warm learning
chat into an awkward transaction and burns the relationship.

## Workflow

### 1 — Who to talk to (and why)
Pick targets with a purpose, from `reconnect-list.md` and 2nd-degree connections:
- **Insiders** at target companies (learn the culture, the team, whether it's real).
- **People in the role you want** (what it's actually like, how they got there — great for a pivot).
- **Connectors** (people who know everyone in the space).
Prioritize warm and 2nd-degree over cold; a mutual connection is the unlock.

### 2 — The ask (hand to network)
Short, specific, low-burden: name the mutual/context, say you admire/are curious about their
{work/path/company}, ask for **15–20 minutes to learn** (not "pick your brain" — be specific about
what). Make it easy to say yes. `network` drafts it in the user's voice.

### 3 — Prepare (respect their time = do your homework)
Research them first (never ask what's on their LinkedIn). Prep 4–6 **specific** questions — see
`references/playbook.md`. Have your own 30-second story ready (from `positioning`) for when they
ask about you.

### 4 — The conversation
Listen more than you talk. Be genuinely curious. Near the end, the two questions that open doors
**without asking for a job**: *"Given what I'm exploring, who else would you suggest I talk to?"*
(referral chain) and *"Is there anything I should be reading/doing?"* Then **always** ask how you
can be helpful to *them* — reciprocity is the point.

### 5 — Follow up (this is where relationships are made or lost)
Thank-you within 24h referencing something specific they said. If they named someone, follow up on
it and **report back** ("you suggested I talk to X — we connected, thank you"). Keep a light touch
over time (share a relevant article, a congrats). Log it in `personal/informational-interviews.md`
so the relationship compounds instead of going cold. `interview-prep` owns the follow-up cadence.

## Feeds the rest of the system
- Company intel and openings you learn about → new leads and rows for `apply-pipeline`.
- New contacts → added to `personal/reconnect-list.md` (the network skill's tracker).
- A warm advocate inside a target company → the strongest possible path when you do apply.

## Guardrails
- **Learn, don't ask.** No job requests, no résumé pushed unless they ask for it.
- **Respect the time.** 15–20 min, do your homework, end on time unless they extend.
- **Reciprocate.** Always offer to help them; relationships aren't one-way extraction.
- **Follow up, always.** The thank-you and the report-back are 80% of the value.
