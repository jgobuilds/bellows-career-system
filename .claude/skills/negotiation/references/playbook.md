# playbook.md — counter framework, scripts, offer comparison

## The counter (structure)
1. **Enthusiasm first.** "I'm excited about this role and the team — I want to make it work."
2. **One clear ask, with a reason.** "Based on the scope and market for this level, I'd like to get the base to $X." Tie the reason to *value/market*, not need.
3. **1–2 secondary levers** so there's room to trade: sign-on to bridge a gap, an equity refresh, a level/title bump, an early comp review.
4. **Then stop talking.** Make the ask and let the silence sit. The next person to speak often concedes.

## Levers, roughly easiest → hardest to move
- **Sign-on bonus** — one-time, off the salary structure, easiest yes; great for bridging a gap.
- **Base** — the compounding number; worth the most long-term. Push here with a market anchor.
- **Equity / RSU refresh** — big at startups/tech; ask for amount + vesting clarity.
- **Level / title** — unlocks a higher band; sometimes easier than moving base within a band.
- **Start date / PTO / remote / learning budget** — soft levers; useful as the "give" that lets them say yes on money.

## Pushback responses (role-play these)
- *"That's the top of our band."* → "I understand. Could we look at a sign-on to bridge it, or an equity/level adjustment?" (move to another lever)
- *"We can't move on base."* → "Understood. Then can we make it right on {sign-on / equity / title / an early review at 6 months}?"
- *"What would it take to get you to yes?"* → give ONE number/package, not a wish list; it usually closes.
- *"We need an answer today."* → "I'm very interested; I want to give it the serious consideration it deserves. Can I confirm by {date}?" (real exploding offers are rare; a little time is normal)

## Never
- Fabricate a competing offer or inflate your current comp. Real leverage only.
- Give an ultimatum you won't honor. If you say "I'd need X to accept," mean it.
- Negotiate against yourself (drop your ask before they respond).
- Accept on the spot out of relief — "thank you, can I confirm by {date}?" is always fine.

## Offer comparison rubric (multiple offers)
Score each on the same axes — total, not just base:

| Axis | What to weigh |
|---|---|
| **First-year cash** | base + realistic bonus + sign-on |
| **Equity** | value at a *sober* valuation + vesting + refresh + risk |
| **Role & scope** | title, level, ownership, team, mandate |
| **Growth** | does it move you along `personal/career-roadmap.md`, or stall you? |
| **Risk** | company stage, runway, manager, reorg exposure |
| **Fit to the *why*** | from career-roadmap — the thing that actually drives you |

The highest base doesn't always win. A role that serves the why and accelerates the roadmap
can beat a richer one that's a detour — but say the trade out loud so it's a choice, not a drift.
