# Prioritization Rubric

Rate every candidate on two axes, then tier. The point is to spend the user's limited energy on the highest-yield contacts first, not to boil the ocean.

## Warmth (drives message tone downstream)
- **Warm** — worked closely, mutual respect, talked within ~2 years, would recognize the name instantly and reply gladly.
- **Lukewarm** — knew each other, positive but not close, or close once but years of silence. The bulk of a "stale" network lives here.
- **Cold** — met briefly, one-way admiration, or a name you know but who barely knows you.

Rate honestly. A message pitched warmer than the real relationship reads false and costs you the contact. When unsure, ask the user "how well do they actually know your work?"

## Usefulness (how well-placed to help)
- **High** — hiring or influential at a target-type company, senior, or exceptionally well-connected in your space.
- **Medium** — in the field, employed somewhere relevant, can refer or point you onward.
- **Low** — out of the field or poorly positioned to open doors.

Usefulness is about their current position, not how much you like them. A beloved former peer now in an unrelated industry is warm-but-low.

## Tiering (priority order)
1. **Warm + High** → contact first. Fast, credible, high-yield.
2. **Warm + Medium** → contact early. Easy reconnects; even when they can't refer, they surface more names ("who else should I talk to?").
3. **Lukewarm + High** → worth the effort; the payoff justifies a slightly more careful reconnect.
4. **Cold + High** → don't approach cold. Route through a mutual for an intro (network's intro-request pattern), or warm them first via visibility/community before asking.
5. **Warm/Lukewarm + Low** → contact opportunistically; good for morale and name-harvesting, low direct payoff.
6. **Cold + Low** → skip. Not worth it.

## Edge cases
- **The over-warm rater.** Users under stress inflate warmth to feel less alone, or deflate it out of embarrassment about the silence. Sanity-check both directions.
- **The "thin network" claim.** Usually under-recall, not true scarcity. Prompting by category (managers / peers / reports / vendors / school) typically triples the list. Don't accept "I don't know anyone" until you've walked the categories.
- **Reciprocity debt.** If the user last spoke to someone by asking a favor and never followed up, that contact is colder than raw recency suggests — factor it in.
- **The silent-too-long worry.** Years of silence is not disqualifying and rarely as awkward as it feels; a genuine, low-pressure reconnect is normal at senior levels. Don't let the user drop good contacts out of embarrassment.
