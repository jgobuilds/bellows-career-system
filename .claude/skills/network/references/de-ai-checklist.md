# De-AI Checklist

The goal: output that reads like a specific human wrote it about their specific career. The strongest de-AI move is upstream — if every bullet uses the user's real, concrete specifics (Phase 1's rule), the writing is already 80% human. Generated smoothness is the tell; lived specificity is the cure. This checklist catches the rest.

## Cadence tells (the biggest ones)
- **Em-dash asides.** AI overuses the "— like this —" interruption. Cut most of them; use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- **The rule of three / tricolons.** "Strategic, scalable, and sustainable." "Built, scaled, and optimized." AI loves triples. Break them — use one strong word, or two, or an uneven list.
- **Uniform sentence length.** AI writes sentences of similar length and rhythm. Vary hard: a long one, then three words. Fragments are fine in bullets.
- **Parallel-structure everything.** Every bullet starting with the same verb pattern in the same shape reads generated. Vary the openings and structures.

## Hollow phrase blocklist (delete or replace with a real specific)
- "results-driven professional," "proven track record," "proven ability to"
- "leveraging," "leverage synergies," "utilize" (→ "use")
- "passionate about," "thrilled at the opportunity," "excited to"
- "dynamic," "innovative solutions," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class," "world-class"
- "spearheaded" (overused to the point of parody — use "led," "started," "ran")
- "in today's fast-paced world," "in an ever-evolving landscape"
- "seamlessly," "robust," "holistic," "impactful"
- "responsible for" (→ show what you did and what happened)
- "team player," "detail-oriented," "self-starter," "go-getter" (show, don't assert)

## Specificity tells
- **Vague magnitudes.** "significantly improved," "greatly increased," "many stakeholders" → use the real number, or if there isn't one, `[NEED METRIC]` and ask.
- **Suspiciously round or too-perfect metrics** across every bullet ("increased X by 50%, Y by 40%, Z by 30%") read fabricated even when true. Keep real precision (37%, not 40%) where the user has it.
- **Claims with no mechanism.** "Drove $2M in savings" is stronger as "Consolidated 3 vendor contracts, cutting spend $2M/yr." The *how* signals it's real.

## Structural tells
- Don't make every section symmetric. Real careers are lumpy — some roles have more to say than others.
- Don't over-hedge or over-qualify. Humans state things plainly.

## The read-aloud test
Read the final output in your head as if speaking it. Where it sounds like a press release or a LinkedIn bot, rewrite in plainer words. The user should sound like themselves — competent and specific, not polished into vapor.

## What NOT to do in the name of "de-AI"
- Don't inject fake typos or forced casualness — that's a different tell.
- Don't strip real structure that aids parsing (standard headers stay; they're for the ATS).
- Don't remove real numbers to seem "humble." Specific results are both human AND effective.
