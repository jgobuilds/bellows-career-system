# Outreach Message Patterns

Each pattern is a skeleton to *personalize per recipient*, never a mass template. The test for every message: could this only have been sent to this one person? If it could go to anyone with the name swapped, rewrite it.

Pull exactly one relevant proof point from `personal/career-profile.md` — enough to establish relevance, never a resume dump.

---

## Cold connection note (LinkedIn, ≤300 characters — hard limit)
Every word counts. No room for warmup.
- Why them (specific), one line on you, soft reason to connect.
- No ask for anything big in the first touch. Connect first, ask later.

Skeleton: `[Specific reason you're reaching out to them] — [one line of genuine relevance from your side]. Would be glad to connect.`

---

## Warm reconnect (former colleague / someone you know)
- Acknowledge the real history honestly (don't overstate it).
- Brief genuine update or reason for reaching out now.
- Soft ask or no ask — reconnecting is legitimate on its own.
- Easy out.

Structure: reconnect line → what you're up to / exploring → light ask (or "no ask, just reconnecting") → easy close.

---

## Referral ask (someone inside the target company)
The highest-value, highest-trust ask. Make it easy for them to say yes AND easy to say no.
- Name the specific role and why you're a genuine fit (one proof point).
- Make the ask explicit and bounded: refer you, OR point you to the right person, OR tell you honestly if it's not a fit.
- Give them the out: "totally understand if you're not comfortable / don't know the team."
- Attach or offer the tailored resume so they can act with one click.

Structure: context (found the role, why it fits) → one proof point → clear bounded ask → easy out → offer to make it frictionless (resume ready).

---

## Intro request (asking a mutual to connect you to a third person)
- Make it forwardable: write it so they can paste it straight to the third person.
- State clearly who, why, and what you're asking of the third person.
- Give the mutual an easy out and a pre-written blurb they can forward.

Structure: the ask to the mutual → a short forwardable blurb (who you are, why you want to meet X, what you're asking) → easy out.

---

## Informational interview request
- You want to *learn*, not to ask for a job. Say so, mean it.
- Specific topic you want their view on (shows you researched them).
- Small, bounded time ask (15-20 min).
- No hidden job ask — that damages trust. If a role comes up naturally later, fine.

Structure: specific reason you want *their* perspective → the narrow topic → bounded time ask → easy out.

---

## Recruiter reply (inbound interest)
- Respond promptly and warmly; they contacted you.
- Confirm genuine interest and the key facts they'll screen on (level, location, authorization, availability) to clear knockouts early.
- One or two lines on fit; offer the tailored resume and times to talk.

Structure: thanks + genuine interest → the facts that clear knockouts → brief fit → next step (resume, availability).

---

## Hiring-manager direct
- Higher risk, can work at senior levels. Be respectful of their time.
- Lead with a specific insight or genuine reason you're excited about *their* team/problem.
- One strong proof point of relevance.
- Small ask: a short conversation, or "who's the right person to talk to."

Structure: specific reason for reaching to them → one proof point → small ask → easy out.

---

## Universal rules
- **Recipient first, user second.** Open with why them, not "I am a data leader with 10 years..."
- **One ask, bounded and easy to grant.**
- **Give an out** in every message — it raises reply rates and protects the relationship.
- **Match temperature** to the real relationship. Never fake closeness.
- **Follow-up etiquette:** one polite follow-up after ~a week is fine; more is pestering. Provide a follow-up only if the user asks.
- **Never** mass-personalize with mail-merge tokens. If the user wants volume, give them a framework to personalize fast, not a blast template.
