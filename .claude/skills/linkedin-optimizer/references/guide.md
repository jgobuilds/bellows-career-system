# guide.md — LinkedIn section best practices

## Headline (220 chars)
- Formula: `{target title / what you are} | {value / what you're known for} | {keywords / stack}`.
- Lead with the searchable role words from `userconfig.TARGET_TITLES` / `LANE_*` — recruiters search these.
- Then a positioning line a human wants to click. Avoid "seeking opportunities" as the whole headline.
- Example shape: `Head of Data & Analytics | Turning messy data into trusted, self-serve capability | Governance · Platform · AI enablement`

## About (2,600 chars; first ~2 lines show before "see more")
- **Front-load the hook** — the first two lines must earn the click.
- First person, the user's voice (`personal/writing-style.md`).
- Arc: hook → what you do and for whom → 2–3 proof points with real numbers → positioning/"why" → soft CTA ("open to talking about {X}").
- Short paragraphs / line breaks; LinkedIn walls of text die.
- No buzzword mush; concrete beats "passionate results-driven leader."

## Experience
- Consistent titles/dates/employers with the résumé (verification).
- Per role: a one-line framing of scope, then 2–4 strongest outcomes (real metrics).
- Slightly warmer/narrative than résumé bullets is fine; keep substance identical.

## Skills & endorsements
- LinkedIn weights your **top pinned skills** in search — choose them deliberately.
- Pin the modern, on-target skills (the `LANE_*` terms); move legacy/dated skills down or off.
- Solicit endorsements on the pinned few (quality > 50 random). Ask specific people for specific skills.

## Featured
- Pin 1–3: a portfolio piece, a strong post, a talk, or the résumé PDF. Visual + credible.

## Recommendations
- 3–5 quality > many. Ask specific people and **prompt them** ("could you speak to how I {did X}?") so they're specific, not generic.

## Settings / signals
- **Open to Work:** recruiters-only (private, safe if employed) vs. the public green banner (max visibility, but everyone sees it). Advise based on whether they're employed and discreet.
- **Custom URL:** clean it up (linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname).
- **Location + industry:** set to the *target* metro/industry so you surface in the right searches.
- **Title consistency:** the current-role title on LinkedIn should match the résumé exactly.

## Honesty
Everything traces to `personal/career-profile.md`. LinkedIn is public and networked — an inflated
claim here is seen by everyone who might vet a warm intro, and it's the easiest place to get caught.
